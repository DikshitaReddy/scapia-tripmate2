# Secondary research notes

These are directional desk-research inputs, not primary user evidence. The assignment brief explicitly asks for evidence from real people; conduct interviews and replace the placeholder examples in your eventual submission with actual notes/quotes.

## Research signals

1. **Group trip planning involves social coordination, not just booking.** A peer-reviewed study on group holiday planning collected 261 usable questionnaires from two university campuses in the UK and China. It explored how individual factors and group characteristics influence intragroup interaction during trip planning. This supports investigating group coordination as a distinct problem, but it does not prove that calendar conflict is the biggest pain point for your target segment.
   - Source: Li, X. et al. (2017/2018), “A Human Factors Approach to Exploring the Experience of Group Trip Planning from the Perspective of Intragroup Interaction,” *International Journal of Human–Computer Interaction*.
   - https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/10447318.2017.1387425

2. **Scapia's current product proposition is already tied to travel bookings and rewards.** Scapia's public website promotes flights, stays, buses and trains through its app, Scapia Coins, and travel benefits. This makes a group-planning layer strategically adjacent to the existing travel journey. It does not establish that Scapia currently supports this exact group-calendar product.
   - Source: Scapia official website.
   - https://www.scapia.cards/

3. **Rewards mechanics should fit the existing ecosystem rather than imply a new cash product.** Scapia's public site describes Scapia Coins and travel redemption. This prototype therefore uses fictional “Travel Club points” for community contribution and flags any future conversion to Scapia Coins, cashback or cash as an assumption requiring validation, legal review and commercial approval.
   - Source: Scapia official website and Federal Bank's Scapia product page.
   - https://www.scapia.cards/
   - https://www.federal.bank.in/scapia

## Product implications (inference, not established fact)

- The hypothesised wedge is **“find the dates that work for the group with the least leave pain”**, rather than another itinerary or group chat.
- Calendar integrations should share free/busy information rather than event titles and details, with explicit consent, to minimise privacy concerns.
- The prototype uses a transparent sample ranking and makes its assumptions visible. A real optimiser should let the group choose between best overall overlap, least leave used, and weekend-first options.
- Flight alignment is a secondary feature. It becomes useful after the group agrees on date windows, especially when members start in different cities or time zones.
- Destination recommendations and community stories can create a post-date-confirmation loop; Scapia booking/rewards integration would need confirmation with Scapia and relevant partners.

## Evidence still required before submission

Interview 5–8 people who have planned a trip with friends while working/studying in different cities. Ask:
- Tell me about the last trip that was difficult to schedule. What happened, in order?
- How did you gather everyone’s availability and leave constraints?
- What tools did you use, and where did the process break?
- How many times did dates change? Did anyone drop out?
- What did the organiser do that others did not have to do?
- Have you ever paid to avoid this problem? Would you pay, and who should pay?
- Would you share free/busy calendar data if event titles were hidden?

Record participant context and exact quotes with consent. Do not invent quotes or claim these interviews happened unless they did.


## Prototype decision after narrowing scope

The refreshed prototype makes group date selection and paid-leave trade-offs the primary journey. Flights follow date selection. Travel Club remains visible as a secondary community experience, while points/rewards are placed under Account. This is a product-scope decision, not evidence that users have validated the community or rewards model. Validate those parts separately through interviews and usability testing.
